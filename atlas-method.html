How the atlas places a mission

The moving positions in the solar view come from NASA JPL SPICE kernels. The renderer is Three.js in Chromium/WebGL. A separate Cesium globe handles body-fixed surface imagery and pins. This separation keeps a lunar or Martian latitude from being mistaken for a Sun-centered coordinate.

What is sampled

The Windows D: server keeps the raw DE440s planetary kernel, NAIF leap-seconds kernel, and archived MER1 cruise kernel. A repeatable build extracts compact position and velocity samples. Apollo is sampled every six hours, Viking every 24 hours, and Opportunity every 12 hours. The moving display uses cubic Hermite interpolation between stored position and velocity states; the original SPK is the authoritative trajectory, and the browser view is for storytelling, not navigation. Opportunity's spacecraft line ends at the last kernel-covered sample. Viking 1 Orbiter uses its reconstructed VO1 SPK, sampled hourly relative to the Mars-system barycenter in J2000. The separate orbit viewer shows a 48-hour window, preserving the actual orbital plane.

Body symbols are enlarged so they can be seen at solar-system distance. Their positions and orbital planes come from the kernels; their on-screen sizes do not represent true diameters. Surface tiles are map mosaics, not guaranteed object-level photographs.

Engine review

OpenSpace is a strong native astronomy visualizer with planetary imagery and mission scenes. Celestia is another mature native space viewer. Their desktop-first workflows would make the requested Windows-server-to-browser delivery harder. Cosmolabe and JSMARS demonstrate useful browser SPICE and planetary mapping patterns. This foundation uses Three.js for the solar scene and CesiumJS for the two surface globes, with NASA SPICE as the source of geometry. Neither renderer is trusted to invent ephemerides.

Foundation build: 15 November 2026. The mission catalog is structured so later stories can add validated events, site pins, orbit SPKs, imagery and narration without changing the coordinate contract.
